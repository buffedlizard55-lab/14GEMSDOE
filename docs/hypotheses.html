H1 · Structural completion: the small connecting faults the catalogue omits

Layers. The USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue geometry itself (training labels raster + INGENIOUS quaternary fault shapefiles v2) and the 1 m DEM (scarp texture inside corridors only). Features: distance/azimuth to nearest trace, along- vs across-strike position, endpoint (tip) distance, overlapping-tip detection with bridging-corridor rasters, intersection/junction density, local lineament-vs-catalogue strike angle (gems/features.py — all seven catalogue-geometry features of the standing prompt).

Physical signature. Relay ramps and step-overs: two sub-parallel strands, overlapping or underlapping tips 0.5–6 km apart, strikes within ±35°. Horsetail splays: splay corridors radiating from tip points. Intersections: junction clusters with connecting minor faults. In the Great Basin inventory these settings host ~32% (step-overs), ~22% (terminations), ~22% (intersections) and ~8% (accommodation zones) of geothermal systems (Faulds et al. 2012 NZ Geothermal Workshop — paper, GDR 383; figures refine to 25/20/18/7% in the later 313-system catalogue of Faulds & Hinz 2015 as summarized in Geoenergy 2023). Those connecting structures are dominated by small faults — precisely the class regional catalogues omit.

Why it catches missing faults. The catalogue records the major strands. The hypothesis is geometric: the omitted faults are the connectors and splays the strand geometry predicts. Every emission is by construction at distance > 0 from the visible catalogue (the model's context excludes them), so the feature “distance to known fault = 0” cannot be the answer — the hide-and-recover protocol enforces it (gems/hide_recover.py, leak assertion every epoch).

Differs from prior work. The team's ledger (run names reported in the project thread) shows DEM-scarp thinning (h20), context-ridge (H25), dotted-ridge (h28) and NMS-thinned catalogue emissions (r7-nms3) — all single-feature emission or thinning of catalogue-adjacent fields. None constructed relay/bridge geometry from the catalogue itself, and none trained through whole-component hiding. This repo's H1 is the first geometry-completion model in the family.

External data. None required. Optional enrichment: INGENIOUS Quaternary faults v2 (direct download, 5.85 MB, free, CC BY 4.0) — obtainability confirmed on the official GDR page 2026-09-28.

Validation gate. Hide-and-recover where the hidden set is biased to connector components (components whose endpoints neighbour two other components); pass = beats the flat/catalogue-emission baseline on spatially-blocked mean DTI. Only then may it use slot #1.

H2 · Magnetic lineaments: concealed faults under basin fill

Layers. GeoDAWN reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly, TMI slopes (vertical/horizontal), top-of-crustal magnetic-source depth (all in the 19-band stack); 1 m DEM detrended elevation where it exists.

Physical signature. Structure-tensor / edge transforms (structure_tensor_orientation): linear ridges of gradient magnitude and coherent orientation, source-depth discontinuities, and truncations of magnetic textures — the classic geophysical expression of a fault contact offsetting magnetic lithologies. Target transform: coherence-weighted edge magnitude plus orientation continuity (Hough-like line support along strike).

Why it catches missing faults. A concealed fault under basin fill has no scarp and no catalogue expression — but it still offsets magnetic sources. Lineaments with strong magnetic coherence that lie away from catalogue traces are fault candidates that only the geophysical expression supports; the experts' own survey (GeoDAWN, sponsored for exactly this purpose) is the likely evidence base for the hidden test faults, which staff have declined to detail (forum 11527).

Differs from prior work. The r7/h20/H25/h28 runs used DEM topography (scarps, ridges) and catalogue thinning. No ledger run applied structure-tensor lineament extraction to the magnetic bands, and none scored strike-mismatch against the catalogue strike field.

External data. None (bands are provided). The raw GeoDAWN release is also public: 10.5066/P93LGLVQ.

Validation gate. Lineament fields built from training-region magnetics must recover held-out block traces at higher DTI than the H1 geometry features alone; the delta is the gate number.

H3 · Hydrothermal-expression gaps: where hot water escapes without a mapped fault

Layers (all free, official — verified obtainable 2026-09-28). INGENIOUS regional compilation (GDR 1391, DOI 10.15121/1881483, CC BY 4.0): paleo-geothermal features (sinter/tufa; 82 KB), well & spring temperature/chemistry (19.9 MB), 2 m temperature probes (1.0 MB); heat-flow maps (10.5066/P9BZPVUC); Quaternary volcanic vents (9.4 MB).

Physical signature. Spring/sinter alignments (springs and sinter mounds localize on permeable fractures), warm-well anomalies, shallow temperature anomalies, vent alignments (dike-fed vents mark crustal structure). The targeted transform is expression-without-explanation: thermal and hydrogeochemical anomalies whose upslope/back-azimuth corridor contains no catalogue trace.

Why it catches missing faults. A hot spring or sinter deposit needs a permeable pathway; ~39% of Great Basin systems are blind with no surface expression at all (GBCGE structural-controls project, DE-EE0002748 final report), so every visible expression is precious evidence of structure — and where the catalogue shows none, the structure is unmapped by definition.

Differs from prior work. No ledger run used any hydrothermal expression layer; the competition stack contains none of these layers (its conductivity, strain and seismicity bands overlap other INGENIOUS products but not heat flow / springs / sinter / vents).

External data / obtainability. All listed above are direct downloads or DOI releases confirmed present today; run scripts/download_external_data.sh thermal on an unrestricted machine (the Arena sandbox blocks binary downloads at TLS level — flagged, not silently skipped).

Validation gate. Enrichment statistic: hidden-trace pixels within 300 m of expression points vs. their share of the valid area, computed on held-out blocks; pass = enrichment ≥ 2× at p<0.01 and positive DTI delta when the expression prior is added to H1.

H4 · Slip/dilation-tendency corridors

Layers. “Quaternary Faulting Slip and Dilation Tendency” (10.5066/P9YL58W6, official ScienceBase release linked from GDR 1391; free) + catalogue strike field; optionally the INGENIOUS geodetic shear/dilation grids (52 MB).

Physical signature. Slip tendency (τ/σₙ) and dilation tendency under the regional stress field: orientations that are frictionally favoured to slip or to open. Physical modelling of Great Basin settings shows step-overs and horsetails concentrate dilatation and Coulomb shear traction (Geoenergy 2023 conclusions) — the same settings H1 targets, now with a stress prior.

Why it catches missing faults. The tendency field is computed from stress + assumed geometry; where the prescribed favourable corridor has no catalogue strand, either the geometry is incomplete or a strand is missing — and the catalogue is the known-incomplete side of that comparison.

Differs from prior work. No ledger run used the tendency product; prior ensembles were purely geomorphic/geomagnetic.

External data / obtainability. Verified DOI exists and is free; download via the ScienceBase item page (binary download must run outside the sandbox — flagged).

Validation gate. A/B on holdout: identical H1 emission with and without tendency weighting; pass = mean DTI delta > 0 across folds.

H5 · Catalogue-completeness residuals

Layers. Strain-rate tensors (dilatation, shear, second invariant — in-stack), isostatic gravity + slope (in-stack), detrended elevation + slope (in-stack), range-front geometry from the 1 m DEM (USGS 3DEP tiles via the provided 1m_DEM_links.csv).

Physical signature. The completeness angle of the standing prompt: predict expected fault density from strain rate, relief and range-front geometry; compute residual = observed catalogue density − predicted; strongly negative residuals that coincide with independent geophysical/topographic evidence (magnetic lineaments, scarp texture) are gap candidates.

Why it catches missing faults. Catalogue density records where mapping effort went, not where deformation is; strain and relief are mapping-effort-free. Their disagreement is the signature of omission.

Differs from prior work. Prior runs used DEM texture directly as emission evidence; none modelled expected vs. observed catalogue density or emitted from residual gaps.

External data. None beyond the provided DEM links.

Validation gate. Hide-and-recover at block scale with whole regions masked as “unknown catalogue”; residual-gap emission must beat uniform corridor emission inside the masked regions.

Protocol common to all five — hide-and-recover (enforced in code)

Every epoch hides a random share of whole catalogue traces (connected components, never pixels), recomputes all context features from the visible remainder, asserts the anti-leak invariant (no hidden pixel may touch the context — assert_no_leak), trains against the full catalogue, and scores DTI on the hidden traces only. Model and hyperparameter selection uses that hidden-recovery DTI; submission decisions use the spatially-blocked variant. This is exactly what the standing prompt requires, and it is why “distance to a known fault = 0” can never be the learned answer.
