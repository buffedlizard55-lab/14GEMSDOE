Round 5 — five new hypotheses, gated on the real rasters

Rounds 1–4 were gated on a synthetic forward model. This round is the first measured on the official competition rasters, with hide-and-recover component folds, a held-out threshold calibration, and the official distance-weighted Tversky metric (no submission slot is spent under any outcome).

The metric rule that reframes every arm. DTI = T/(T + 0.2E + 0.8·FN), so adding one unit of value at a pixel whose kernel credit is k raises the score iff k > 0.2·DTI. At our operating point (0.1563) that is k > 0.031: a prediction 1 px from a true trace still earns k = 2/3 and one 2 px away still earns 1/3, both far above the bar — because a false positive costs only 0.2 while a miss costs 0.8. Two consequences: emit supports at full value, and let a believed support travel with its 1–2 px kernel neighbourhood. Both forms are asserted numerically in tests/test_dti_fast.py.

Gate protocol (pre-registered before any arm was scored)

  1. Truth: the official catalogue (training_labels.tif); 3,199 connected components.
  2. Split per fold: TEST 20 % / CALIB 20 % / HIDE 35 % of all components, leaving 25 % always visible. Features for training and for choosing the emission threshold are built from the visible quarter only, so a hidden structure is absent from every context-derived channel (hide-and-recover). The scored field is then rebuilt with the full catalogue minus TEST — exactly what a submission-time model sees.
  3. Calibration: the emission threshold is chosen on the hidden CALIB traces only, never on TEST.
  4. Learner: identical for every arm (HistGradientBoostingClassifier, 120 iterations, lr 0.08, 31 leaves); only the feature block changes.
  5. Truth protocols: dense (all TEST pixels), sparse (fixed 20 % subsample), far (TEST pixels more than 1,000 m from any context trace — the closest analogue of a genuinely unmapped fault, and the protocol that cannot be won by hugging the catalogue).
  6. Emission sweep: thresholds × dilations (0/1/2 px) and budgets {2, 5, 10 %} of the footprint × {top-k, greedy NMS r = 3}.
  7. Decision rule: promote only if an arm beats the baseline on both sparse and far in ≥ 3 of 4 folds with the calibrated threshold; everything else is killed and recorded.

What the interim record says

  • R5-4 (range-front profile curvature) is the strongest single addition measured in this project to date and the only round-5 arm that beats the baseline on both the sparse and the far protocol in both completed folds (+0.0064 dense and +0.0044 far on fold 0; +0.0048 dense and +0.0056 far on fold 1).
  • All five mechanisms improve the far protocol — TEST pixels more than 1 km from any other catalogue trace, the closest available analogue of a genuinely unmapped structure. Three of them lose the sparse comparison in fold 1, i.e. in a linear model they dilute as often as they help.
  • The calibrated budget is ~0.5 % of the footprint — 6× smaller than the 155,021-px support the group historically uploaded. Support quality, not mass, is what the metric pays for.
  • Absolute levels (≤0.045 dense) are hide-and-recover catalogue numbers on a 20 % component split; they are not leaderboard estimates and must not be compared with the 0.1563 plateau.

Promote rule (unchanged, pre-registered): beat geom on sparse AND far in ≥ 3 of 4 folds. With two folds available, nothing is promoted and nothing is killed; the rerun is the first action of the next session.
