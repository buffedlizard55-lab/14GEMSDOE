Round-3 hypotheses — the completeness angle, continuation cones, magnetic fabric, scarplet linkage

Round 2 found that a multiplicative blend of classifier × geophysical prior beats every single-field idea. Round 3 asks the next question: are there structures and transform families nothing in the repo has ever computed — catalogue-geometry continuation, catalogue-vs-geology residuals, magnetic fabric, scarplet connectivity — that change the verdict? Each hypothesis names its layers, its physical signature, why it targets a fault missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue, and how it differs from everything already implemented. All were gated on the spatially-blocked holdout before any submission slot. Sources inline, opened 2026-09-28; full record in research/hypotheses_round3.md.

Gate verdict (2026-09-28, synthetic forward model — see honesty note). No round-3 candidate beat the incumbent (BMUL 0.3842 combined DTI; best challenger BMUL-R3A 0.3790, −0.0052). All five candidates and both incumbent-stack variants were killed. The follow-up prior test was exactly null (+0.0000) and yielded this session's most useful mechanism finding: the multiplicative prior can only re-weight the classifier's support — it can never extend it, so any "field X through the prior" proposal is dead unless X overlaps the classifier's confident pixels. No slot was spent. The gate has now killed 6 round-2/3 arms with literature support — that is it working.

The null that teaches something. The multiplicative prior scales the classifier's own probability: at a bridge pixel where the classifier says ~0, (1 + 0.5·prior)·0 stays 0; where the classifier is ~1 the product clips at 1 anyway. The prior re-weights the classifier's support; it cannot extend it. Any future proposal of the form "add field X to the prior" must first demonstrate pixel-level overlap with the classifier's confident support — otherwise it is null by construction. This single measurement retires an entire family of would-be ideas cheaply.

What was fixed along the way (audit trail)

  • Run 1 of the gate was voided: a unit test caught a coordinate-frame bug that emptied the R3A cones on axis-aligned traces (_outward_direction); fixed and pinned by tests/test_hypotheses_round3.py.
  • Run 1's incumbent was also wrong in our favour: round-2's BLEND-MUL trains its classifier on the ALL block, and the first re-run compared candidates against a weaker baseline-only incumbent. validate_round3.py now reproduces the exact round-2 emission (baseline recovery matches round 2 to 4 decimals, pinning protocol equivalence).
  • Honesty: all numbers are the synthetic forward model, as in round 2. They validate the machinery and the sign of each effect — not a leaderboard score. The script runs unchanged on the real rasters once data/raw/ is populated (one command; see Data & sources).
