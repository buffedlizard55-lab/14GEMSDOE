Scoreboard context (public leaderboard, verified 2026-09-28): leader DARD 0.3168, then alexoktaba 0.2993, HardcoreTechGod 0.2854. Our group's best public numbers sit at 0.1563 / 0.1461 / 0.1193. §1 Why 0.1563 kept happening

  1. Published builders show matching metadata. GEMSDOE1 and 5GEMSDOE pages display the same truncated artifact-hash prefix, encoded-run count and histogram. This is evidence their site builders present the same field, not proof of which file was uploaded; original files and platform submission IDs are unavailable in this repo.
  2. Rounded score ties do not prove duplicate predictions. The public scores are shown to four decimals. Different rasters can produce the same displayed DTI; cross-account ties must not be called shared work without pixel-array hashes.
  3. Historical artifacts are missing. The ledger contains repeated shortened artifact IDs, including entries with blank scores. These are flagged for review, not classified as duplicates without full hashes or upload records.
  4. Real-data model comparison is blocked. Synthetic holdouts and leaderboard scores are different evidence and are not directly comparable. Competition rasters/labels are absent, so no new feature arm can be promoted for a slot yet.

Current safeguards: submission manifests record both TIFF and prediction-array hashes. Exact pixel-array repeats are blocked; rounded score collisions are warnings only. A candidate may be considered for a slot only after spatially-blocked hide-and-recover evaluation on authorized real competition data beats the real-data incumbent. Synthetic validation is machinery-only and cannot qualify a submission.

§2 The metric arithmetic (official equations, transcribed)

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w + ε)

TP_w = Σ_{g∈GT}  max_{x : d(x,g)≤300m}  p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FN_w = Σ_{g∈GT}  1 − max_{x : d(x,g)≤300m}  p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FP_w = Σ_{x : p(x)>0}  p(x)·[1 − max_{g∈GT} k(d(x,g))]
k(d) = max(1 − d/300m, 0)

Consequences we design against:

  • Recall is 4× precision. A missed hidden fault costs β·(1−p·k); a stray pixel of equal magnitude costs α·p·(1−k). Emission breadth on geologically plausible corridors is cheap; silence is expensive.
  • Per-GT-pixel max. Credit for each ground-truth pixel is the best single prediction near it — a thin line at p≈1 on the trace beats a diffuse cloud at p≈0.3, which pays FP everywhere and still leaves FN high.
  • 300 m tolerance is small at 100 m pixels — 3 pixels. Off-trace accuracy of ±1 px is worth 2× ±2 px. Line geometry matters.
  • Catalogue pixels are free but worthless (masking ruling). Emission should concentrate where the catalogue is not.
  • Phase 2 rewards belief. Predictions experts verify become labels and are re-scored as truth ($250k phase). Well-reasoned novel corridors have option value beyond the public score.

§3b Round 2 — what the gate changed (2026-09-28)

The round-2 hypotheses are listed on the round-2 page. What the blocked holdout decided, and what it means for strategy:

FindingConsequence for how we build a submission
A multiplicative blend of classifier and geophysics is the only blend that helps. classifier × (1 + 0.5·prior) scored +0.0979 combined DTI and improved recovery and discovery. Max and additive blends were strongly negative. Never smear probability mass to buy recall. The metric credits each ground-truth pixel by its single best prediction within 300 m, so breadth costs FP without lifting that maximum. Keep traces thin and boost them where independent evidence agrees.
A catalogue-trained model is structurally bad at discovery (baseline discovery DTI 0.0081), and discovery is the entire competition because known-fault pixels are masked. Training labels drawn only from the catalogue optimise a population that contributes nothing to the score. Every gain came from injecting information from outside the catalogue (geophysics, seismicity, topography).
The best-supported hypothesis lost. Gravity-gradient edge terminations had a verbatim statement from the INGENIOUS authors behind them and still scored −0.0059 as a classifier feature. Literature support is not evidence about our feature set. The gate is the only arbiter, and it must be allowed to kill favourites.

§4 Slot discipline (the 3-per-week budget)

  1. Slot rule: an idea may only use a slot after it beats the current holdout best and its delta is written down (what changed, expected mechanism, holdout before/after).
  2. Slot roles: #1 = current best believed field; #2 = the strongest challenger; #3 = a deliberate diagnostic (e.g. the catalogue-hedge probe that measures how masking is implemented) whose interpretation is written before it is uploaded.
  3. Final selection: the single file for both rounds is chosen for Phase-2 upside (genuine novel structure), not just public LB noise.
  4. Never again identical by accident: scripts/check_submission_uniqueness.py compares content hashes, filenames, NOTE/MANIFEST provenance, manifest integrity and the ledger's duplicate scores/artifacts, and must exit 0 before any upload. The historical GEMSDOE1/5GEMSDOE duplicate is reproduced and caught in tests/test_submission_uniqueness.py.

§5 What we explicitly will not do

  • Re-ship the 7f00890a… field or any direct derivative of it under a new name — the hash check makes this mechanical.
  • Treat training-catalogue DTI as evidence of anything except sanity.
  • Spend slots on ablations whose outcome is predictable from the metric (e.g. wider emission bands at fixed geometry — the FP/β arithmetic already answers those).
  • Chase the public leaderboard's private-test variance; the one file that matters is selected at the deadline, blind to private scores (rules §3.6.2).
  • Promote hypothesis N4 (1 m lidar scarps + paleo-shoreline mask) before a free, official, machine-readable paleo-shoreline dataset has been sourced (irregularity #1b). Until then it is recorded as blocked, not as progress.
  • Report a round-2 holdout number as a leaderboard prediction. It is a synthetic-forward-model result; it validates the machinery and the sign of each arm's effect, nothing more.
